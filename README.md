# USA-Regional-Impact
COVID-19 affected counties across the United States unevenly, with the highest number of cases concentrated in large metropolitan areas. Using county-level data, this analysis examined which counties experienced the greatest number of confirmed cases and how the virus spread geographically across the country. Bar charts were used to identify the counties with the highest numbers of confirmed case counts and the highest death rates, while a geographic bubble map was used to visualize the distribution of cases across the United States.

The analysis showed that counties with large urban populations, such as New York County and Los Angeles County, experienced the highest number of confirmed COVID-19 cases. This pattern is likely due to higher population density, increased travel activity, and greater opportunities for viral transmission. The death rate analysis showed that some counties experienced higher mortality rates relative to their total case counts, which may reflect disparities in healthcare access, differences in population age, or variations in local response strategies. The geographic visualization further demonstrated how COVID-19 spread across the United States, with early hotspots appearing in densely populated urban regions before spreading to other parts of the country.

The time series analysis showed that confirmed COVID-19 cases increased steadily over time. The data also revealed a consistent weekly reporting pattern, likely caused by differences in weekend and weekday reporting. Forecasting models suggested that, if trends continued, the number of cases would keep rising during the observed period. One limitation of this dataset is that it does not include population size or healthcare capacity, which could help explain why some counties were more affected than others.

Therefore, the results suggest that COVID-19 had the greatest impact in highly populated urban counties and that geographic spread was strongly influenced by population density and regional connectivity. These findings highlight the importance of geographic analysis in understanding public health crises and identifying areas that may be most vulnerable during future outbreaks.
